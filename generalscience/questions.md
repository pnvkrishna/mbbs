## 1. Confidentiality – When, With Whom, and How Can It Be Breached?

### Definition
Confidentiality is the ethical and legal duty of a doctor to protect patient information.

### When Can It Be Breached?
1. **With patient consent**
2. **Legal obligation**
   - Court orders
   - Notifiable diseases (TB, COVID, etc.)
3. **Public interest**
   - Risk to others (e.g., communicable diseases, violence)
4. **For patient care**
   - Sharing within healthcare team
5. **Administrative purposes**
   - Insurance (limited information)

### With Whom?
- Treating healthcare team
- Legal authorities
- Public health authorities
- At-risk individuals (ethically justified cases)

### How to Breach Properly?
- Share **minimum necessary information**
- Inform patient (if possible)
- Maintain documentation
- Follow legal protocols

### Ethical Principles
Autonomy, Beneficence, Non-maleficence, Justice

## 2. Breaking Bad News (SPIKES Protocol)

### Definition
Delivering information that negatively impacts a patient’s future.

### Steps (SPIKES):
- **S – Setting:** Ensure privacy and comfort
- **P – Perception:** Assess patient understanding
- **I – Invitation:** Ask how much they want to know
- **K – Knowledge:** Deliver information clearly
- **E – Empathy:** Address emotions
- **S – Strategy:** Plan next steps

### Key Points
- Avoid jargon
- Give information gradually
- Allow silence
- Provide emotional support

## 15. Types of Consent & Informed Consent

### Types of Consent
1. **Implied consent** – Routine examination
2. **Express consent**
   - Oral
   - Written
3. **Informed consent** – Mandatory for procedures
4. **Proxy consent** – For minors/unconscious patients

### Informed Consent – Components
- Nature of procedure
- Risks and benefits
- Alternatives
- Consequences of refusal

### Valid Consent Criteria
- Competence
- Voluntariness
- Adequate information

## 19. Medical Negligence – Ethical, Legal & Professional Issues

### Definition
Failure to provide standard care resulting in harm.

### Elements (4 D’s)
- **Duty** – Doctor-patient relationship
- **Dereliction** – Breach of duty
- **Damage** – Harm caused
- **Direct causation** – Link between breach and harm

### Types
- Civil negligence
- Criminal negligence

### Prevention
- Good communication
- Proper documentation
- Following standard guidelines

### Ethical Aspect
- Non-maleficence
- Accountability
